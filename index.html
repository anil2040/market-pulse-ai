📊 Macro Insights · what this morning's news adds up to

  1. September CPI printed 3.7% year-over-year, hotter than forecast, with core inflation ticking up to 2.6%; wholesale inflation (PPI) also surprised on the upside, marking the fastest pace since 2022 and signaling inflation is re-accelerating rather than cooling as hoped.
  2. Fed rate hike odds for December surged to 86% on the inflation data, and October hike odds jumped to 35% from 22% before the report; Fed Governors Bowman and Hammack speak today and markets are parsing every word for signals on whether tightening continues this month or waits until December.
  3. CoreWeave, the heavily indebted AI infrastructure company, saw shares plummet 15% and bond yields spike above 15% as investors question whether AI capex is being funded recklessly; semiconductor stocks are down 12% in six sessions and chip names remain under pressure premarket with Nvidia, AMD and Micron all declining.
  4. The middle class is showing visible stress: department stores and fast-casual restaurants frequented by younger, salaried workers are struggling while discount retailers like Dollar General, pawnshops, and warehouse clubs are seeing new traffic from households that used to spend more freely.
  5. CEO confidence collapsed to 46 in a Conference Board survey (anything below 50 is negative) as 41% now expect economic conditions to worsen over the next six months, yet CEOs have not meaningfully cut spending or hiring plans, suggesting the gloom has not yet translated into corporate pullback.
  6. Bank stocks took a beating yesterday on regulatory pressure over alleged political debanking and a historic $1.2 billion anti-money laundering fine against Bank of America; large banks are caught between a deregulatory administration and a president willing to publicly attack the industry over cultural issues.
  7. Energy prices remain elevated with diesel especially high and a container ship attacked in the Strait of Hormuz yesterday; Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries, undercutting Trump's recent energy ceasefire and keeping supply concerns alive through the midterm election.
  8. Retail sales and jobless claims reports today will test whether the consumer is truly slowing or whether the K-shaped bifurcation is simply shifting within income brackets; retail sales ex-auto and initial claims are the key data points to watch.
  9. Netflix announced 6% workforce cuts (roughly 1,000 employees) citing slower-than-desired growth, signaling that even secular growth names are rightsizing as financing costs rise and multiple expansion stalls across consumer discretionary.
  10. What could shift the picture: a dovish surprise from Fed speakers today, a material miss on retail sales or jobless claims this morning, or any sign that the AI funding stress extends beyond CoreWeave into the hyperscaler tier itself.
  11. Dashboard read: The macro heat score at 77/100 signals overheating and rising stress across credit, rates, and valuations; a flat yield curve (37 bps) combined with a CAPE of 41.4x (more than double the 17x historical average) means duration risk and valuation compression remain severe headwinds even as earnings hold up; 10Y Treasury at 5.21% and HY spreads widening to 316 bps reflect investors repricing recession and credit risk, while headline inflation at 3.7% trending up and core at 2.8% confirms the Fed has more work to do, all of this pressure on tech and growth stocks, combined with middle-class demand softening and CEO confidence collapsing, leaves a deep-value investor vulnerable to being early on cyclicals but positioned correctly to wait for genuine distress in quality names at rational prices.
